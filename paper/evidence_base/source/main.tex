\title{Beyond Mean Foils: Auditing Worst-Foil Specificity in Frozen CLIP Region Explanations}
\input{authors.tex}

\begin{abstract}
A frozen CLIP region can pass locality and mean-foil screens while responding
more strongly to a non-target foil. We audit the maximum-target-drop single
region from CCI candidates using matched prompt-mean and aggregate worst-foil
margins. On COCO/OpenAI B/16, 41.18\% of eligible images pass both screens yet
have negative worst-foil margin; 64.38\% [63.67,65.07]\% of screen-passers fail.
Selection-stage target-tolerant reranking at
$\epsilon=.02$ switches 2.0--2.6\% of images and improves normalized aggregate
margin by $.0150/.0108/.0118/.0087$ across COCO/VOC and B/16/B/32.
All specificity-gain 10,000-resample paired confidence intervals exceed zero;
held-out target-response intervals include zero. Gains persist with
category-disjoint and annotation-absent foils. Margin improvement differs from
sign repair: COCO B/16 negative-margin prevalence falls only from 74.04\% to
73.89\%, while switched-image bbox precision falls by 4.95 percentage points
on average. Reranking demonstrates sparse local margin improvement within
frozen candidates, not broad failure elimination or loss-free explanations.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
CLIP, explainability, interventions, model auditing, worst-foil specificity
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
Frozen vision-language models such as CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} are often interpreted by ranking image regions
according to the change in image--text similarity after an intervention.
CCI~\cite{agarwal2025cci}, for example, clusters CLIP patch tokens into regions
and combines cluster importance into a heatmap. We audit only its candidate
region with maximum target-score drop (CCI-top1), not the complete heatmap. A large drop says
that a region matters for the target; it does not establish that the effect is
\emph{specific} to the target. Removing generic object evidence can reduce a
competing semantic foil even more, while bounding-box overlap remains high.
Likewise, averaging over many easy foils can hide one high-impact confounder.
On COCO B/16, 41.18\% of eligible images have bbox precision $\ge .5$ and
positive aggregate mean-class margin, yet negative worst-foil margin.
Among the 17,725 screen-passers, 11,411 (64.38\%) fail the worst-foil audit.

We audit the declared foil-response vector of the \emph{selected} frozen
region and report target response, worst-foil specificity, and spatial locality
separately. The contribution is the post-selection audit and controlled evidence
of local margin improvement, not the max/CVaR operator itself~\cite{rockafellar2000cvar}.
COCOA~\cite{lin2023cocoa} defines a corpus-versus-foil attribution objective;
CASE~\cite{williamson2025case} diagnoses class-insensitive saliency and builds
contrastive explanations. Here the object is a fixed selected region's
intervention-response vector: we test what locality and mean-class screens miss,
then probe improvement with selection separated from held-out audit.
Related CLIP attribution~\cite{wang2023m2ib,li2023clipsurgery,dreyer2025cliplatent},
class/concept contrasts~\cite{li2026cdaclip,cheng2026mgaclip,visser2026contrastiveconcept},
and caption foils~\cite{yuksekgonul2023aro} motivate this distinction.
Image Description Enhanced CLIP-Adapter (IDEA)~\cite{ye2026idea} combines visual
features with image descriptions for few-shot classification. Our audit instead
examines target specificity of selected regions with the model held fixed.

Reranking is a controlled probe of local improvement, not the principal
algorithmic claim. Negative relative specificity does not imply that the region
provides no faithful evidence for the target: evidence may support several classes.

\section{Worst-Foil Audit and Target-Tolerant Reranking}
For checkpoint $m$, $s_m(x,c)=\tau_m\langle z_m(x),e_m(c)\rangle$ uses
unit-normalized embeddings and the checkpoint's learned
$\tau_m=\exp(\mathrm{logit\_scale}_m)$, before softmax.
For candidate region $R$ and concept prompt $c$, define
\begin{equation}
\dropv_R(c)=s_m(x,c)-s_m(\mathcal I(x,R),c),
\label{eq:drop}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I$ is a frozen internal intervention. Our single-region CCI baseline
selects
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm CCI}=\arg\max_{R\in\regions(x)}\dropv_R^{\rm sel}(t),
\label{eq:cci}
\end{equation}
using only the selection prompt. CCI-top1 denotes this baseline;
the CCI subscripts and figure labels retain this shorthand.
For non-target foils $\foils_t$, a worst-foil
margin compares the target response with the strongest foil response.

\paragraph{Matched held-out estimands.}
Selection uses ``a photo of a \{category\}''. Held-out evaluation uses
``a picture of the \{category\}'', ``an image containing a \{category\}'', and
``the \{category\}''. Let $e_h(c)$ denote the stored unit-norm text embedding
for held-out prompt $h$, and
$n_c=\|H^{-1}\sum_h e_h(c)\|_2$.
We apply the same class-specific normalization rule,
$\widetilde d_h(c)=d_h(c)/n_c$ and~define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\max}^{\rm pm}(R,t)
&=H^{-1}\!\sum_h[\widetilde d_h(t)-\max_f\widetilde d_h(f)],\\
P_{\max}^{\rm agg}(R,t)
&=H^{-1}\!\sum_h\widetilde d_h(t)-
\max_f H^{-1}\!\sum_h\widetilde d_h(f).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:estimands}
\end{equation}
Thus $P_{\max}^{\rm pm}\le P_{\max}^{\rm agg}$: the former permits a different
hardest foil per template. Both are ontology-relative specificity measures.
For the mean-class screen, write $\bar d_c=H^{-1}\sum_h\widetilde d_h(c)$.
The archived screen uses $P_{\rm mean,all}=\bar d_t-|\mathcal C|^{-1}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\bar d_c$
over all dataset classes, equally weighted, on these same held-out responses.
Excluding the target gives $P_{\rm mean,foil}$, with
$P_{\rm mean,all}=(|\mathcal C|-1)P_{\rm mean,foil}/|\mathcal C|$;
their positive screens coincide. This class average differs from prompt-mean.

\paragraph{Selection-stage target tolerance.}
For each candidate define
\begin{equation}
M^{\rm sel}(R,t)=\dropv_R^{\rm sel}(t)-
\max_{f\in\foils_t}\dropv_R^{\rm sel}(f).
\end{equation}
Given tolerance $\epsilon$, form
\begin{equation}
\regions_\epsilon=\{R:\dropv_R^{\rm sel}(t)\ge
\dropv_{R_{\rm CCI}}^{\rm sel}(t)-\epsilon\},
\end{equation}
and rerank only this feasible set:
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm WF}(\epsilon)=\arg\max_{R\in\regions_\epsilon}M^{\rm sel}(R,t).
\label{eq:repair}
\end{equation}
Because $R_{\rm CCI}\in\regions_\epsilon$, the feasible set is nonempty;
any switch loses at most $\epsilon$ of the \emph{selection-stage} target drop.
This is not a held-out target guarantee. Equation~\ref{eq:repair} reads only
selection-stage tensors, and held-out prompts are used only after the region is
fixed. We use $\epsilon=.02$ as a fixed operating point, not an optimized value.
Its cosine-scale tolerance is $.02/\tau_m$, not necessarily equal across checkpoints.
Figure~\ref{fig:method} summarizes the separation.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\paragraph{Models, intervention, and endpoints.}
We use $K=8$ frozen CCI regions on COCO val2014~\cite{lin2014coco}
(27,708 eligible images) and VOC2007 test~\cite{everingham2010voc} (1,943), with
OpenAI CLIP B/16 and B/32. Reranking changes only which frozen candidate is
selected. Each image has one target: the largest-area annotated instance's
category (COCO annotation area; VOC box area). Eligibility requires another
annotated category and target-instance area between 2\% and 80\% of the image;
COCO crowd annotations are excluded. Full foils comprise every other dataset
category (79 for COCO, 19 for VOC). The intervention blocks selected patch key/value columns in every
visual-transformer block and head by adding $-\infty$ before attention softmax,
while retaining the prefix token. Cached patch features are clustered by
K-means ($n_{\rm init}=3$, 50 iterations, seed $1701+i$ for cache index $i$).
BBox precision is the fraction of selected patch centers in the union of
transformed boxes of the target category, not only its largest instance, and is
reported separately because perturbation metrics depend on the intervention
operator~\cite{gomez2022metrics}. Ties use the first exact NumPy argmax.

\paragraph{Held-out controls and uncertainty.}
Selection uses one template and evaluation uses the three held-out templates in
Eq.~\ref{eq:estimands}. Cross-foil controls use ten fixed seeds
$1701,2701,\ldots,10701$ to permute categories into equal alternating folds.
Each direction selects using one fold and audits on the other, excluding the
target from both. The 20 seed--direction deltas are averaged within each image
before paired unique-image resampling;
annotation-absent evaluation removes categories annotated as present without
claiming semantic absence. Fixed 5/19-foil subsets probe cardinality and composition jointly.
Intervals use 10,000 paired unique-image bootstrap resamples; the archive fixes
the base seed at 1701 and records deterministic setting/metric-specific seeds.
Only four provenance-valid OpenAI settings enter the direct reranking analysis;
two historical LAION settings are excluded because their text-normalization
provenance is unresolved.

\paragraph{Worst-foil debt.}
On COCO/OpenAI B/16, CCI-top1 has normalized
$P_{\max}^{\rm agg}=-.342$ [--.357,--.327], and 74.04\% [73.56,74.61]\% of
selected regions have negative aggregate margin. Its held-out raw target
response is $.939$ [.924,.953] and bbox precision $.679$ [.674,.683].
These are full-sample means, not estimates within the negative-margin subset.
Table~\ref{tab:screens} decomposes the screen failure. Within the negative-margin
subset, mean raw target drop is $.481$ and 90.40\% have positive target drop.

\paragraph{Sparse margin improvement.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the direct baseline comparison. Normalized aggregate
and prompt-mean deltas are positive with CIs above zero in all four settings;
the corresponding prompt-mean deltas are +.0150, +.0108, +.0119, and +.00867.
Held-out target-response intervals include zero in all four; this does not
establish equivalence or a no-loss guarantee. Locality is not
uniformly preserved: B/16 on COCO and VOC shows small bbox decreases, while B/32
intervals include zero.

\paragraph{The gain is not merely selector--endpoint reuse.}
Figure~\ref{fig:ident} shows that the gain persists under category-disjoint
cross-foil and annotation-absent evaluation. Pooled normalized aggregate gains
are +.01052 [+.00901,+.01198], +.00728 [+.00629,+.00846], +.00705
[+.00296,+.01140], and +.00438 [+.00131,+.00784] for COCO B/16, COCO B/32,
VOC B/16, and VOC B/32. Restricting evaluation further to annotation-absent
foils remains positive on the full eligible image population: +.00939, +.00697,
+.00809, and +.00443, with all paired CIs above zero. These controls support
gain persistence, not baseline failure-rate persistence under the same restrictions.

\paragraph{Descriptive same-feasible-set comparisons.}
In Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, WF has larger point estimates than uniform,
target runner-up, and mean-foil selection; Max-.1 nearly matches WF.
These are gains relative to CCI-top1, not paired WF-minus-alternative CIs,
so they do not establish statistically significant superiority.

\paragraph{Why switching is sparse.}
At $\epsilon=.02$, COCO B/16 has 26,405 singleton feasible sets;
1,215/80/8 images have 2/3/4 candidates. Only 1,303/27,708 (4.70\%)
permit an alternative, and 663/1,303 (50.88\%) switch.

\paragraph{The effect also reduces the breadth of foil violations.}
As a secondary held-out diagnostic, let
$\bar d(c)=H^{-1}\sum_h\tilde d_h(c)$ for the already fixed region.
We report $V_{\rm rate}$, the fraction of foils with $\bar d(f)>\bar d(t)$,
and $V_{\rm mass}=|\foils_t|^{-1}\sum_{f\in\foils_t}[\bar d(f)-\bar d(t)]_+$,
averaging over all foils, including zeros.
On COCO/OpenAI B/16, reranking at $\epsilon=.02$ changes $V_{\rm rate}$ from
$.18973$ to $.18751$ and $V_{\rm mass}$ from $.05878$ to $.05708$.
Violation breadth therefore also decreases descriptively.

Across the three held-out phrasings, mean pairwise hardest-foil identity agreement is

use the same all-three criterion, not the pairwise criterion.
Families follow the frozen metadata mapping, e.g., vehicle and animal.

\paragraph{Cases and uncertainty.}
Figure~\ref{fig:cases} selects an annotation-absent-foil gain, a case near the
switched-gain 75th percentile, and the worst switched loss; it does not estimate frequencies.
Added screen intervals use 10,000 image-bootstrap draws (seed 1701);
conditional mean intervals resample the 663 switched images (seed 1702).
\paragraph{Interpretation and scope.}
Inference concerns eligible multi-category images under fixed templates,
foils, candidates and intervention; bootstrap does not establish robustness
to new configurations. Missing annotations need not imply semantic absence.
The screen decomposition is verified for COCO B/16; other settings and
restricted-foil baseline conditional rates remain unreported.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
CCI-top1 auditing exposes relative-specificity failures and sparse margin
improvement, not broad sign repair. Held-out target response and locality
remain separate endpoints; WF superiority is not established.
