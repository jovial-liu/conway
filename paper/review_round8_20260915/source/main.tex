\title{Beyond Mean Foils: Auditing Worst-Foil Specificity in Frozen CLIP Region Explanations}
\input{authors.tex}

\begin{abstract}
Spatial overlap and mean class contrast need not imply target-specific region
responses. We audit CCI's maximum-target-drop region in frozen CLIP, using
separate prompts for selection and evaluation. Across COCO and VOC with two
checkpoints, 41.08--64.78\% of locality/mean-foil screen-passers fail a worst-foil
criterion. With original screening memberships retained, excluding annotated-present
foils leaves 39.69--63.64\% failing. Exclusion reduces failure beyond exact
cardinality-matched random removal by 0.86--0.92 percentage points on COCO;
VOC intervals include zero. Positive-target filtering retains high failure.
Among screened failures, unrestricted eight-region oracles permit sign repair
in 6.25--7.84\% on COCO and 27.40--31.15\% on VOC. A target-drop tolerance
of $\epsilon=.02$ reduces feasible repair to 0.16--0.98\%; relaxing it increases
opportunity with setting-dependent target and locality changes.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
CLIP, explanation auditing, class specificity, constrained region selection
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} region explanations use intervention-induced
image--text score changes. Cluster-based Concept Importance
(CCI)~\cite{agarwal2025cci} groups patches and measures their contribution;
we audit its maximum-target-drop region, CCI-top1. A positive mean-foil
contrast can average away a strong competitor: masking a region may reduce
the target score more than the average non-target score, yet reduce one
non-target score even more. Worst-foil specificity tests this strongest
competitor, complementing spatial overlap and mean contrast.

Wang and Wang~\cite{wang2022whynot} analyze mean, max, and weighted
class contrast. Their max contrast chooses the non-target class with the
highest original-image logit; our worst foil maximizes the score drop under
a region intervention and can vary with the region. CCI-top1 isolates the
region selected for maximum target drop, enabling a direct audit of its
cross-class specificity.

COCOA~\cite{lin2023cocoa} explains corpus similarity relative to foil samples;
CASE~\cite{williamson2025case} tests class sensitivity and constructs
contrastive saliency. Our contribution combines fixed-population auditing,
exact cardinality-matched foil controls, and candidate/budget decomposition
to quantify worst-foil failure and decompose constraints on sign repair.
Related CLIP attribution~\cite{wang2023m2ib,li2023clipsurgery,dreyer2025cliplatent},
class contrasts~\cite{li2026cdaclip}, and caption foils~\cite{yuksekgonul2023aro}
provide complementary perspectives.

\section{Audit and Repair Opportunity}
Figure~\ref{fig:method} links candidate construction from frozen patch
features (a), one-prompt selection (b), and three-prompt evaluation of
the fixed region (c).

Let $x$ be an image, $t$ its target class, and $c$ any dataset class.
A candidate $R\in\regions(x)$ is a set of image patches; $\regions(x)$
contains $K=8$ candidates. For checkpoint $m$, the pre-softmax score is
$s_m(x,c)=\tau_m\langle z_m(x),e_m(c)\rangle$. Here $z_m$ and $e_m$ are
unit-norm image and prompted-text embeddings, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$
is their inner product, and $\tau_m$ is CLIP's learned positive logit scale.
The intervention $\mathcal I(x,R)$ masks attention to patches in $R$.
The resulting score drop is
\begin{equation}
d_R(c)=s_m(x,c)-s_m(\mathcal I(x,R),c).
\label{eq:drop}
\end{equation}
Positive $d_R(c)$ means a score reduction. Superscript $\mathrm{sel}$
denotes the prompt ``a photo of a \{category\}''. CCI-top1 selects
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm CCI}=\arg\max_{R\in\regions(x)}d_R^{\rm sel}(t).
\label{eq:cci}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Held-out margins and screens.}
Evaluation uses ``a picture of the \{category\}'', ``an image containing a
\{category\}'', and ``the \{category\}''. Index these $H=3$ templates by $h$. Let $e_h(c)$ be their unit-norm
text embeddings and $d_{R,h}(c)$ the drops from Eq.~\ref{eq:drop}. Define
$n_c=\|H^{-1}\sum_h e_h(c)\|_2$ and the normalized aggregate
$\bar d_c(R)=H^{-1}\sum_h d_{R,h}(c)/n_c$. We suppress $R$ when fixed.
A foil is a non-target class; $\foils_t$ contains all such dataset classes.
The held-out worst-foil margin is
\begin{equation}
P(R,t)=\bar d_t-\max_{f\in\foils_t}\bar d_f.
\label{eq:estimands}
\end{equation}
This equals using normalized mean text embeddings; class-specific $n_c$ can
change rankings. Tables report raw target drop $H^{-1}\sum_h d_{R,h}(t)$,
whereas the positive-target screen uses $\bar d_t$. Selection and evaluation use separate templates.

Let bbox precision be the fraction of selected patch centers inside
target-class boxes. For CCI-top1, the fixed baseline screen-passers are
$A=\{\mathrm{bbox}\ge .5,\ \bar d_t-\mathrm{mean}_f\bar d_f>0\}$ and the failure set is $F=\{P<0\}$. The mean is over $f\in\foils_t$;
$A$ requires at least half of patch centers to overlap target boxes and
a positive target-minus-mean-foil drop. To distinguish positive mean-foil
contrast from positive target contribution, we also audit
$A^+=A\cap\{\bar d_t>0\}$. The primary sign-repair population is
$B=A\cap F$, fixed before reranking. Throughout, ``failure'' denotes $P<0$, and ``sign repair'' denotes a
transition from $P<0$ to $P\ge0$.

\paragraph{Target-constrained diagnostic probe.}
In Figure~\ref{fig:method}(b), $M^{\rm sel}$ measures selection-stage
target-minus-worst-foil drop. The tolerance $\epsilon\ge0$ defines the
feasible subset $\regions_\epsilon\subseteq\regions(x)$:
\begin{equation}
M^{\rm sel}(R,t)=d_R^{\rm sel}(t)-\max_{f\in\foils_t}d_R^{\rm sel}(f),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\regions_\epsilon=\{R:d_R^{\rm sel}(t)\ge d_{R_{\rm CCI}}^{\rm sel}(t)-\epsilon\},
\label{eq:feasible}
\end{equation}
and worst-foil (WF) reranking
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm WF}=\arg\max_{R\in\regions_\epsilon}M^{\rm sel}(R,t).
\label{eq:wf}
\end{equation}
CCI is feasible by construction. We use $\epsilon=.02$ logit units
and sweep $.01/.02/.05/.10/.20$ within each checkpoint.

\paragraph{Exact diagnostic oracles.}
For each image $i$, write $P_i(R)=P(R,t_i)$ for its target $t_i$.
An oracle checks every candidate using held-out responses to count available
repairs. With $\mathbf1\{\cdot\}$ the indicator (one if true, zero otherwise),
the feasible sign-repair count on fixed baseline $B$ is
\begin{equation}
N_O(B,\epsilon)=\sum_{i\in B}\mathbf1\!\left\{
\max_{R\in\regions_\epsilon(i)}P_i(R)\ge0\right\}.
\label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
The unrestricted oracle searches all $K$ candidates. A sign pass means
$P_i(R)\ge0$, without locality or positive-target constraints. Failures split
into: no passing candidate ($C_0$); passing candidates but \emph{none feasible}
($C_1$); a feasible pass missed by WF ($C_2$); and WF sign repair ($r$).
Thus $|B|=C_0+C_1+C_2+r$ and $N_O=C_2+r$. We additionally count repairs
satisfying $J(R)=\{P_i(R)\ge0,\ A(R),\ \bar d_t(R)>0\}$; $A(R)$ applies
the same full-foil screen to the new region, without changing fixed baseline $B$.
Here $r$ counts WF sign repairs and $j$ counts those also satisfying $J$.
Oracles measure available opportunity; WF selects using only its selection prompt.

\paragraph{Fixed-population foil controls.}
For image $i$, let $\foils_i^{\rm abs}$ remove all annotated-present categories
from the non-target foils. The protocol holds $R_{\rm CCI}$ and full-foil screen $A$ fixed;
$F_i^{\rm abs}$ tests $\bar d_t-\max_{f\in\foils_i^{\rm abs}}\bar d_f<0$.
Reducing the foil set weakly decreases failure, so we also match its cardinality. For $N_i$ full
foils, $k_i=|\foils_i^{\rm abs}|$, and $m_i$ foils with $\bar d_f>\bar d_t$,
a uniform size-$k_i$ subset without replacement fails with probability
\begin{equation}
q_i=1-\frac{\binom{N_i-m_i}{k_i}}{\binom{N_i}{k_i}}.
\label{eq:matched}
\end{equation}
With zero numerator when $k_i>N_i-m_i$, we compare
$\Delta_A=|A|^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}(\mathbf1\{F_i^{\rm abs}\}-q_i)$.
Restricted sets are nonempty, and crowd annotations are excluded.

\section{Protocol and Reproducibility}
COCO val2014~\cite{lin2014coco} and VOC2007 test~\cite{everingham2010voc}
provide 27,708/1,943 eligible images, each evaluated with OpenAI CLIP B/16
and B/32 (ViT-B with 16- or 32-pixel patches; 59,302 image--model records). The target is the largest instance's
category, using annotation area on COCO and box area on VOC. We require
another category and 2--80\% target area; COCO crowd annotations are excluded.
Full foils are the other 79/19 classes.

K-means clusters L2-normalized projected patch features: $K=8$, three
initializations, 50 iterations, seed $1701+i$ (sorted IDs shuffled with seed
1701 define $i$). Intervention $\mathcal I$
adds $-\infty$ to attention logits at selected patch-key columns before
softmax in every block/head, retaining the class-token column. Ties use first argmax; MPS features are cast to float32.
Target boxes are mapped by $224/\min(W_{\rm img},H_{\rm img})$ and center-crop offsets,
where $W_{\rm img},H_{\rm img}$ are the image width and height~\cite{gomez2022metrics}.

\paragraph{Failure persists after annotation exclusion.}
The fixed screen $A$ contains 17,726, 17,848, 1,209, and 1,227 images
in table order. Exclusion lowers failure by only 1.14--1.39 percentage
points of $A$; 96.63--98.24\% of fixed baseline failures $B$ remain
(Table~\ref{tab:screens}). Table~\ref{tab:screens} separates this change from foil-count reduction:
COCO exclusion has a larger effect than random removal of the same number
of categories, whereas VOC $\Delta_A$ intervals include zero.
The residual failure extends beyond the contribution of explicitly
annotated co-occurring classes.

\paragraph{Positive-target and scene-count checks.}
The mismatch persists under positive-target filtering. Among $A^+$,
full-foil failure is 62.64--63.79\% on COCO and 40.66--41.40\% on VOC.
After annotation exclusion, it remains 61.38--62.62\% and
39.26--40.05\%, respectively. Matched-random comparisons retain
the COCO/VOC interval pattern. Even scenes with exactly two annotated
categories have COCO annotation-absent failure of 63.39/64.95\%.

\paragraph{Candidate and budget bottlenecks differ by dataset.}
Table~\ref{tab:oracle} shows that no candidate passes ($C_0$) for
92.16--93.75\% of COCO screened failures $B$,

\paragraph{Budget relaxation helps in every setting.}
Table~\ref{tab:frontier} reports oracle capacity $O_B=100N_O/|B|$ and
achieved repair $r_B=100r/|B|$. The changes $\Delta d_t$ and $\Delta b$
are WF minus CCI averages over all eligible images, with bbox change
expressed in percentage points. At $.02$, 95.21--95.60\% of images have only one feasible candidate
(Table~\ref{tab:frontier}); WF has little room to change the region.
At $.20$, oracle sign-repair capacity rises to 1.57--2.05\% of COCO
$B$ and 6.65--7.14\% of VOC $B$.
Raw-target changes become negative in all four settings, but bbox changes
are negative on COCO and positive on VOC. Budget relaxation therefore has dataset-dependent locality effects.
Each comparison uses the same checkpoint and fixed baseline population.

\paragraph{Normalization has a small effect on the fixed audit.}

\paragraph{Which images are averaged?}
Table~\ref{tab:frontier} averages over all eligible images, including
unchanged regions. Table~\ref{tab:costs} averages only over WF sign
repairs within fixed baseline $B$.

\paragraph{Target and locality changes after sign repair.}
Table~\ref{tab:costs} shows negative mean bbox changes in all four
repair subsets: $-14.04$ to $-28.17$ percentage points. Target-score
changes are positive on COCO and negative on VOC. Thus crossing the
worst-foil threshold does not impose a common direction on target response
or spatial overlap. In every setting, some sign repairs fail to retain the locality/mean-foil
screen and positive target contribution ($j<r$ in Table~\ref{tab:costs}). The joint
feasible-oracle counts are 12/26/3/2.
Outside the screened population, sign transitions can also regress:
COCO B/32 has four opposite-sign regressions among all baseline passes.
Counts over all failures or all switched regions have different
denominators from the screened sign repairs reported here.

\paragraph{Failure persists at larger margin thresholds.}
At $\delta=.10$, 59.49--60.22\% of COCO and 34.39--36.23\% of VOC
screen-passers $A$ still fail (Table~\ref{tab:threshold}). Rates fall further at
$.50$, so the magnitude and threshold matter. The threshold sweep characterizes the magnitude of failure within
the normalized aggregate score.

\paragraph{What the comparisons establish.}
Table~\ref{tab:screens} isolates the effect of removing annotated-present
classes from the automatic benefit of reducing the foil count.
Table~\ref{tab:oracle} and Figure~\ref{fig:ident} then separate candidate
availability from budget feasibility: a better reranking rule alone can
recover only the additional opportunities in $C_2$ at the fixed budget.
Table~\ref{tab:frontier} shows how enlarging that feasible set changes the
attainable repair rate. Together, these comparisons locate the main
constraints on sign repair in the candidate set and target-drop tolerance.

\paragraph{Records and uncertainty.}
The primary tables use one traceable release of candidate masks, selection
responses, three-template held-out responses, and bbox records generated
together. Checks verify patch partitions, bbox recomputation, response
aggregation, candidate choices, and the failure decomposition.
\href{https://github.com/jovial-liu/conway/tree/7ee2b6080aa6752790e17c910c5c8a3111235d4d/experiments/priority123_20260915}{The frozen release} includes candidate summaries, validation reports,
configuration, and hashes; masks and full responses are retained locally.
Table~\ref{tab:normalization} retains earlier-run regions and population;
three images changed selected regions between runs.

Confidence intervals (CIs) use 10,000 image bootstraps and percentile
2.5/97.5 limits, pointwise without multiplicity correction. Paired effects
are differenced within image before resampling. Foil comparisons resample
within fixed $A$ or $A^+$; repair-cost intervals condition on actual
repairs. Exact $q_i$ is primary; 100 seeded draws check it.

\section{Conclusion}
Locality and positive mean-foil contrast leave substantial worst-foil failure
in CCI-top1 regions, persisting after annotation exclusion and at larger
margin thresholds. Candidate availability and target-drop constraints
account for most limits on sign repair. Larger budgets increase capacity
with setting-dependent target and locality effects. These findings support
reporting worst-foil specificity alongside locality and mean-foil contrast
when evaluating frozen CLIP region explanations.
