ABSTRACT
A frozen CLIP region can pass locality and mean-foil screens
while responding more strongly to a non-target foil. We audit
the maximum-target-drop single CCI region using matched
prompt-mean and aggregate worst-foil margins. In the frozen
COCO B/16 archive, 41.18% of eligible images pass both
screens yet fail the worst-foil audit; 64.38% [63.67,65.07]%
of screen-passers fail. Target-tolerant reranking at ϵ = .02
switches 2.0–2.6% of images; paired margin gains persist un-
der category-disjoint and annotation-absent foils. Independent
four-setting MPS reruns add direct paired gains over Mean
and Max-.1, but gains over Max-.1 are small. Switched-image
bbox precision drops by 4.95 and 13.20 percentage points on
COCO and VOC B/16. Margin improvement is sparse, differs
from sign repair, and does not imply loss-free explanations.
Index Terms— CLIP, explainability, interventions, model au-
diting, worst-foil specificity

INTRODUCTION
Frozen vision-language models such as CLIP [1] are of-
ten interpreted through intervention-induced image–text score
changes. CCI [2] clusters patch tokens and combines clus-
ter importance into a heatmap. We audit only its maximum-
target-drop region (CCI-top1). A large target drop need not be
target-specific: generic object evidence can support another
class more strongly, despite high box overlap. Averaging easy
foils can hide this failure. In the frozen COCO B/16 archive,
11,411 of 17,725 locality/mean-class screen-passers (64.38%)
have negative worst-foil margin.
Our contribution is the selected-region response-vector
audit and controlled evidence of local improvement, not the
max/CVaR operator [3]. COCOA [4] defines corpus-versus-
foil attribution; CASE [5] diagnoses class-insensitive saliency
and builds contrastive explanations. We test what locality and
mean-class screens miss, then separate region selection from
held-out audit. Related CLIP attribution [6, 7, 8], class/concept
contrasts [9, 10, 11] and caption foils [12] motivate this distinc-
tion. Image Description Enhanced CLIP-Adapter (IDEA) [13]
combines visual features and descriptions for few-shot classi-
fication; our audit keeps the model fixed. Reranking probes
local improvement. Negative relative specificity does not im-
ply absent target evidence: one region may support several
classes.

EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
Models, intervention, and endpoints.
We use K = 8 frozen
CCI regions on COCO val2014 [14] (27,708 eligible images)
and VOC2007 test [15] (1,943), with OpenAI CLIP B/16
and B/32. Reranking changes only which frozen candidate
is selected. Each image has one target: the largest-area an-
notated instance’s category (COCO annotation area; VOC
box area). Eligibility requires another annotated category
and target-instance area between 2% and 80% of the image;
COCO crowd annotations are excluded. Full foils comprise
every other dataset category (79 for COCO, 19 for VOC).
The intervention blocks selected patch key/value columns in
every visual-transformer block and head while retaining the
prefix token. BBox precision is the fraction of selected patch
centers inside transformed target boxes and is reported sepa-
rately because perturbation metrics depend on the intervention
operator [16]. Ties use the first exact NumPy argmax.
Held-out controls and uncertainty.
Selection uses one tem-
plate and evaluation uses the three held-out templates in Eq. 3.
Cross-foil controls use ten fixed seeds 1701, 2701, . . . , 10701
to permute categories into equal alternating folds. Each direc-
tion selects using one fold and audits on the other, excluding
the target from both. The 20 seed–direction deltas are averaged
within each image before paired unique-image resampling;
annotation-absent evaluation removes categories annotated as
present without claiming semantic absence. Fixed 5/19-foil
subsets isolate foil-cardinality effects. Intervals use 10,000
paired unique-image bootstrap resamples; the archive fixes
the base seed at 1701 and records deterministic setting/metric-
specific seeds. Only four provenance-valid OpenAI settings
enter the direct reranking analysis; two historical LAION set-
tings are excluded because their text-normalization provenance
is unresolved.

cases, not frequencies. All 59,302 rerun image–model records
pass strategy/array checks. CPU/MPS smoke checks cover
only 1–2 images per setting.
Interpretation and scope. Rerun and archive are separate
evidence sets. COCO B/16 image IDs align, but three im-
ages change screen/failure status and ten change CCI region
labels. Some response changes exceed threshold rounding;
frozen candidate tensors are unavailable for causal attribution.
COCO B/32 switches 614 times versus 613 in the archive,
whose missing per-image fields prevent localization. These
differences are not attributed to MPS or treated as exact repro-
duction. Inference is conditional on eligible images, templates,
foils, candidates and intervention. Restricted-foil conditional
failure rates and robustness to new candidate or intervention
configurations remain untested.
Conclusion. CCI-top1 auditing exposes specificity failures
and sparse margin improvement. Independent reruns support
positive paired margin gains over Mean and Max-.1 under the
tested protocol, with small gains over Max-.1 and measurable
locality costs. Target response, locality and sign repair remain
distinct endpoints.
